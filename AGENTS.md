# Skill authoring conventions

These instructions govern maintenance of `SKILL.md` and `references/*.md`, not the wording of research manuscripts produced with the skill.

## Directive language

- **must / must not / do not**: Binding requirements, including scientific accuracy, research integrity, verified venue obligations, and authorized task scope. Use only when the requirement is genuinely non-negotiable in its stated context.
- **should / should not**: Default editorial strategies. Apply when relevant; a concrete reason based on the research, intended reader, supplied manuscript, user request, or verified venue rules can justify departing from them.
- **consider**: Evaluate a technique when its **explicit, observable trigger** applies. Adoption is optional; consideration is not.
- **may**: Permission or a possible alternative. It does not require consideration or adoption.

Use direct procedural imperatives such as *identify*, *compare*, *check*, and *report* for workflow actions; do not mistake those for a separate manuscript-writing level. Descriptive and explanatory sentences need no modal verb.

## Editing discipline

1. Preserve the scientific meaning, safeguards, task scope, and reference-selection routes. Do not weaken a binding restriction merely to shorten the file.
2. Consolidate generic statements about flexibility and non-mandatory templates in `SKILL.md`. Avoid repeating them after every technique.
3. Keep specific triggers, exceptions, evidence requirements, and failure conditions alongside the guidance they govern.
4. Rewrite conflicting or redundant guidance rather than appending a stronger instruction next to it. Keep examples illustrative; they must never imply an invented research component or result.
5. When changing directive strength, verify that the new strength matches the intended obligation in both new drafting and revision. A default writing pattern is not a reason to rewrite an already effective passage.
6. Retain citations, source notes, internal links, and applicability conditions when editing reference material. Review the diff for accidental loss or contradictions.

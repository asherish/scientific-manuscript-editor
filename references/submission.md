# Venue-aware drafting and submission preparation

Venue-aware drafting and submission preparation align **the manuscript's scientific claims, its research stage, and the target venue's actual requirements**. A main-paper draft should fit a realistic venue page budget from the outset while remaining sufficiently complete for scientific appraisal.

## Establish the applicable venue rules

Venue policies determine which presentation choices are mandatory. Before applying submission rules, the editor **must** identify the exact venue, article type, submission stage, current official instructions, and template. Check the applicable main-paper length and what counts toward it, structure, appendix and supplementary-material permissions and length limits, reviewer access to those materials, anonymity, reference, figure, disclosure, and writing-style rules. Keep venue requirements separate from common field conventions or editorial preferences; if a rule cannot be verified, report that uncertainty.

## Budget the main paper and preserve supporting detail

For drafts **before or after experiments**, the main paper **should** fit the verified venue limit while stating the research question, consequential definitions and assumptions, central methodological argument, and the evaluation design or evidence needed to assess its claims. Allocate extra derivations, detailed implementation procedures, planned analyses, and reproducibility information to a **submitted appendix or supplement only when permitted** by the applicable rules. A permitted appendix does not replace the essential explanation and evidence needed in the main paper.

If an appendix or supplement is prohibited or its page budget is insufficient, the editor **should preserve the remaining technical detail in clearly identified non-submitted notes or an internal appendix** for the author's use and future implementation. **Never imply that this material will be submitted or available to reviewers**, and do not cite it as reviewer-accessible evidence. If the omission would prevent scientific appraisal, flag the limitation and request an author decision rather than treating private notes as a workaround. When the venue is unspecified or requirements are unverified, use an author-provided or explicitly provisional budget and label it as such; **do not invent venue limits**. Check the compiled page count when possible, and otherwise report that compliance is unverified.

## Reconcile the manuscript with the completed research

The submitted manuscript must describe the completed work, not merely the original plan. Reconcile procedures, data, settings, deviations, analyses, and findings with the research record. Then check that the title, Abstract, Introduction, and Conclusion communicate the same contribution supported by the Results. Place important uncertainty and scope limitations near the claims they qualify.

Use the relevant section references from `SKILL.md` when substantive exposition needs work. In particular, `results.md` contains guidance for evidence-linked findings, and `discussion.md` covers scientific interpretation and limitations. This file governs venue-aware allocation and submission-stage reconciliation, not the detailed construction of those sections.

## Revise under space constraints without losing meaning

Submission revisions **should** resolve unsupported claims, broken logic, inconsistent definitions, and missing scientific explanation before shortening passages. Then reduce unnecessary repetition and detail that does not help the intended reader, preserving necessary methods, evidence, and qualifications. If the venue's main-text limit conflicts with information needed for validity or reproducibility, identify the trade-off, use permitted supplements where possible, and preserve remaining technical detail for the author without claiming it was submitted.

## Verify the assembled submission

The assembled submission **should** be reviewed with its figures, tables, captions, equations, references, and required supplementary material. Check consistency of terminology, quantities, sample sizes, labels, claims, and the paper's central takeaway. Use `verification.md` for the original-to-revised scientific comparison when an existing manuscript has been edited, and report unresolved policy or author decisions explicitly.

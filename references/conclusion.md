# Conclusion: preserve the paper's defensible takeaway

Use this reference when writing, diagnosing, or substantially revising a Conclusion or closing summary. It should leave the reader with **the problem addressed, the contribution actually established or proposed, and the most consequential implication or remaining limit**.

## Synthesize rather than repeat

Return to the paper's research question and state how the work addresses it. Summarize principal contributions or findings at the level supported by the actual study; do not convert a proposed method into a demonstrated benefit, extrapolate beyond evaluated settings, or introduce a new result at the end. Align wording with the Introduction, Abstract, Problem Statement, Results, and Discussion.

For pre-experiment writing, state the intended contribution and what evidence the planned evaluation is meant to produce, without writing expected findings as though they were observed. For completed research, emphasize supported findings and their bounded significance. Choose tense, voice, length, and any required closing structure according to the manuscript's stage and verified target-venue instructions, not a universal rule.

Identify a material limitation, unresolved question, or future direction only when it genuinely follows from the work. A formulaic future-work sentence is not required. End with a clear scientific takeaway rather than promotional language or an unsupported claim of broad impact.

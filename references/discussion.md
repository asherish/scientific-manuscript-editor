# Discussion: interpret the findings within their limits

Discussion explains **what the findings mean, how they relate to existing knowledge, and where their implications stop**. It advances the interpretation beyond the direct observations reported in Results. Some article types integrate these functions into a combined Results and Discussion section.

## Answer the research question from the evidence

The Discussion **should** begin from the principal findings, explaining how they address the original question or gap. Distinguish what was directly measured or demonstrated from the mechanism, explanation, or practical implication inferred from it. A clear Discussion advances interpretation; it should not merely restate the Results section or introduce an unrelated contribution.

## Develop interpretations around findings and substantive issues

After the opening answer to the research question, organize paragraphs or paragraph groups around important findings or interpretive issues. Each unit **should** identify its finding, interpretation, or limitation early, evaluate it using relevant results and literature, and develop the supported meaning, conditions, or remaining uncertainty.

Combine several results when they support one interpretive issue; separate independent issues even when they arise from the same result. The Discussion need not repeat the Results subsection by subsection. When a condition is necessary to prevent an interpretation from being overstated, explain it alongside that interpretation rather than postponing it to a general limitations passage.

When a discussion passage repeats observations without developing their meaning, or its evidential reasoning is unclear, **consider** a progression suited to its role:

| Explanatory role | Possible progression |
|---|---|
| Position a finding | Important finding → agreement or disagreement with relevant studies → what it adds to existing understanding |
| Explain a possible mechanism | Observation to explain → candidate explanation and supporting evidence → alternatives or unresolved elements → discriminating test where needed |
| Evaluate a limitation | Specific restriction → effect on a particular inference → conclusion that remains justified |
| Draw practical implications | Evidence-grounded implication → application conditions → untested aspects |
| Identify future work | Open question → needed test or data → what that work could resolve |

Select the elements needed for the issue; do not add a speculative mechanism or future experiment merely to complete a progression. Derive practical guidance and future work from the interpretations and open questions actually established. For a pre-experiment manuscript, discuss anticipated interpretive limits and conditional implications of the planned design without presenting hypothetical findings as observed. Use `composition.md` for general paragraph construction.

## Position the interpretation among prior findings

The Discussion **should** compare findings with genuinely relevant literature, including important agreements, disagreements, and differences in study design or assumptions. Explain plausible reasons for contrasting outcomes without treating speculation as established fact. Refer readers back to the evidence rather than introducing unsupported claims about what prior work proves.

## Separate explanation from speculation

Distinguish the observation itself from a proposed mechanism or causal account. A performance difference, association, or example may suggest an explanation without confirming it. Explain which competing causes, confounders, or additional experiments would be needed to test the interpretation. If an insight emerged from exploratory inspection, identify it as such rather than portraying it as a pre-specified confirmation.

## Bound generalization and acknowledge limitations

Limitations should tell readers **which conclusions remain justified under which conditions**. Consider relevant restrictions involving populations, tasks, data quality, model assumptions, uncertainty, or study design, and explain how each affects interpretation. For example, a measured association may support predictive usefulness without establishing that changing one factor would cause the outcome to change. Name the actual limitation and its consequence instead of listing generic weaknesses.

Future work should arise from a real open question, a limitation that can plausibly be addressed, or a testable next step. A realistic practitioner recommendation should similarly state the conditions under which it is expected to hold. Paragraphs should follow the substantive logic of interpretation, limitations, and realistic next steps.

## Give conditional guidance to future users

Where a method has plausible practical users, consider a brief **practitioner's guide**: report evidence-grounded advice on suitable data, preprocessing, meaningful parameter choices, workload assumptions, and conditions where the approach may fail. When application to real data reveals substantive domain observations beyond aggregate scores, explain them with representative and countervailing evidence, not merely a collection of favorable success stories. Do not invent recommendations or observations.

Manchester describes recurring discussion functions; Mensh and Kording and MIT offer writing guidance. The explanatory progressions above synthesize those principles rather than reproduce a prescribed or experimentally validated paragraph sequence. MIT covers a combined closing Discussion/Conclusion; adapt its guidance when these are separate sections.
